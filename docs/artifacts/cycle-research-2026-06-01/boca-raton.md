## Geography (verified)

Boca Raton is a coastal city on the Atlantic Ocean and the southernmost city in Palm Beach County, sitting between the ocean and the Intracoastal Waterway (ICW), with inland communities extending west of I-95. It has roughly two miles of Atlantic beachfront on a barrier island. Lake Boca Raton is a tidal lagoon connected to the ocean via the Boca Raton Inlet at the southern edge of the city. The ICW through the city has no fixed bridges (drawbridges only — e.g., Palmetto Park Road and Camino Real bridges), giving many waterfront homes ocean access for large vessels. [verified]

> Caveat (verified nuance): "No fixed bridges" means no low non-opening bridge on the immediate path to the inlet; vessels may still transit one or more drawbridges. The Boca Raton Inlet is narrow and prone to shoaling (NOAA Chart 11467) — present as a navigational reality, not as a guaranteed deep-draft passage.

## Confirmed Durable Facts (safe to publish)

1. Boca Raton is in Palm Beach County, Florida, and is the southernmost city in the county. [verified — Wikipedia, Britannica]
2. The city lies between the Atlantic Ocean and the Intracoastal Waterway, with roughly two miles of Atlantic beachfront on a barrier island and inland communities extending west of I-95. [verified — Wikipedia]
3. The Boca Raton Inlet connects the Intracoastal Waterway to the Atlantic Ocean at the southern edge of the city. [verified — myboca.us Marine Information; westpalmbeach.wiki]
4. The Intracoastal Waterway through Boca Raton has no fixed bridges (only drawbridges), allowing many waterfront homes ocean access for large vessels. [verified — myboca.us; multiple brokerage navigation guides]
5. Lake Boca Raton is a tidal lagoon connected to the ocean via the inlet. [verified — Wikipedia]
6. Major corridors include I-95, U.S. 1 (Federal Highway), State Road A1A (oceanfront, between the Intracoastal and the Atlantic), Glades Road, Palmetto Park Road, Yamato Road, Military Trail and Florida's Turnpike. [verified — Wikipedia; A1A position confirmed]
7. Architect Addison Mizner shaped the city's identity in the 1920s with Mediterranean Revival / Spanish Colonial Revival architecture; the 1926 Cloister Inn (now The Boca Raton resort) and the Old City Hall are Mizner-era landmarks. [verified — Wikipedia, bocahistory.org] (Cloister Inn opened Feb 6, 1926, on the west shore of Lake Boca Raton.)
8. Mizner Park is a downtown Mediterranean Revival lifestyle center named for Addison Mizner; it houses the Boca Raton Museum of Art and an amphitheater and opened in January 1991. [verified — Wikipedia/Mizner Park] (Amphitheater building opened Nov 2002; now the Count de Hoernle Amphitheater.)
9. Royal Palm Yacht & Country Club (est. 1959) is a guard-gated waterfront estate community with a Jack Nicklaus Signature golf course and a member marina with Intracoastal/ocean access. [verified — rpycc.org, royalpalm.com] (Original course by Robert Trent Jones Sr., later renovated to a Jack Nicklaus Signature course.)
10. The Sanctuary is a 1980s guard-gated yachting community on the west side of the Intracoastal, just north of the Spanish River Boulevard bridge, with a ~20-slip marina and deep-water dockage. [verified — thesanctuaryofbocaraton.com and brokerage pages] (NOTE: one brokerage page described it as "ungated"; the community's own site and the weight of sources confirm guard-gated with 24-hour manned gates — guard-gated stated as a physical amenity only.)
11. East Boca contains numerous mid-century (1950s-60s) single-family canal-front waterfront neighborhoods including Golden Harbour, Boca Harbour, Bel Marra, Caribbean Keys and Lake Rogers Isle. [verified — all confirmed as real Boca Raton waterfront communities]
12. West of I-95, Boca Raton has large gated golf-and-country-club communities including Boca West, St. Andrews Country Club, The Polo Club, Broken Sound and Woodfield Country Club. [verified — multiple sources; all confirmed real Boca Raton communities]
13. Major parks include Spanish River Park, Red Reef Park, Gumbo Limbo Nature Center, Sugar Sand Park and South Inlet Park. [verified — myboca.us; Gumbo Limbo on Wikipedia] (Gumbo Limbo sits at the north end of Red Reef Park.)
14. Florida Atlantic University and Lynn University are located in Boca Raton. [verified — FAU.edu, Wikipedia; both confirmed as separate institutions in Boca Raton]
15. Pearl City, platted in 1915, is one of Boca Raton's oldest residential neighborhoods (now a designated historic district / National Register). [verified — bocaratonobserver.com, bocaratontribune.com]
16. Old Floresta is a historic district of early Mizner-era Mediterranean Revival residences inland west of downtown (Mizner's first subdivision; historic district since 1990). [verified — activerain/Old Floresta district, bocaratonobserver.com]

## Fair Housing Notes (binding for any published copy)

- Do not reference school quality, school ratings, or specific schools as a selling point. (FAU/Lynn may be named as geographic landmarks/institutions, not as a school-quality selling point.)
- Avoid "safe", "safety", low-crime, or security-of-neighborhood framing. Gated/guard-gated may be stated only as a physical amenity, never as a safety claim.
- Avoid "family-friendly", "good for families", "great for raising kids", or any family-status framing.
- Do not characterize residents by demographics — income, age, race/ethnicity, national origin, religion, or "exclusive/prestigious clientele" language about people.
- Describe communities by physical and lifestyle amenities (waterfront, docks, golf, marina, architecture) only — never by who lives there.
- Avoid religion-linked framing or proximity-to-houses-of-worship as a selling point.
- **Cycle-specific corrections:** remove "exclusive" / "ultra-private" / "prestigious" adjectives that imply a class of clientele (appeared on The Sanctuary and St. Andrews descriptions).
