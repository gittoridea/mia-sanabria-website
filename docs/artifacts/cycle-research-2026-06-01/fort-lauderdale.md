## Geography

Coastal city on the Atlantic Ocean roughly 30 miles north of Miami, defined by the Intracoastal Waterway, the New River, and an extensive system of finger-island canals (~165 miles of inland waterways) that give it the nickname "Venice of America." Many neighborhoods east of the Intracoastal offer deep-water dockage with direct or near-direct ocean access via the Port Everglades inlet; inland areas west of the Intracoastal are largely non-waterfront ("dry lot"). `[verified]` — distance ("30 miles north of Miami"), 165 miles of inland waterways, and "Venice of America" all confirmed on Wikipedia; waterway geography and dry-lot/waterfront split confirmed across brokerage and tourism sources.

## Confirmed Durable Facts

- Fort Lauderdale is the county seat and most populous city of Broward County, Florida. `[verified]` — Wikipedia ("seat of government of and most populous city in Broward County").
- It sits on the Atlantic Ocean roughly 30 miles north of Miami. `[verified]` — Wikipedia ("30 miles (48 km) north of Miami").
- It is nicknamed the "Venice of America" for its approximately 165 miles of inland waterways and finger-island canals. `[verified]` — Wikipedia; visitlauderdale.
- The New River runs through the city and connects to the Intracoastal Waterway and, via Port Everglades, to the Atlantic Ocean. `[verified]` — Wikipedia; corroborated across neighborhood sources (Rio Vista / Tarpon River / Sailboat Bend all described on the New River with ocean access).
- The Intracoastal Waterway runs north-south through the city; deep-water canal homes east of it offer direct or near-direct ocean access. `[verified]` — confirmed across waterfront-neighborhood sources (Bay Colony, Coral Ridge "no-fixed-bridge ocean access").
- Port Everglades is the ocean inlet and one of the busiest cruise ports in the United States. `[verified]` — Wikipedia describes it as "the nation's third busiest cruise port." (Dossier's "one of the busiest" is a safe, accurate softening.)
- The city has roughly 7 miles of Atlantic Ocean beaches, with State Road A1A running along the beachfront. `[verified]` — visitlauderdale ("7 miles of beaches"); A1A beachfront corroborated.

## Confirmed Sub-Areas

All 13 sub-areas independently confirmed to exist in or adjacent to Fort Lauderdale. None are inventions; none are misattributed to a different city. Character lines below are physical/lifestyle/amenity only.

- **Las Olas Isles** `[verified]` — Dredged 1920s finger islands off Las Olas Boulevard with deep-water dockage for large yachts; one of the city's iconic waterfront enclaves.
- **Seven Isles** `[verified]` — Compact district of finger islands just north of Las Olas Boulevard with Intracoastal-access canals and waterfront residences.
- **Rio Vista** `[verified]` — Established waterfront neighborhood with tree-lined streets, historic homes, and canal lots with ocean access via the New River.
- **Colee Hammock** `[verified]` — Historic neighborhood along the New River near Las Olas Boulevard, known for restored Mediterranean Revival and Colonial Revival homes.
- **Idlewyld** `[verified]` — Small waterfront neighborhood near the New River and Las Olas Beach with canal-front homes.
- **Harbor Beach** `[verified]` — Gated waterfront community near Port Everglades with wide deep-water canals, private dockage for large yachts, and private beach access plus its own beach club and marina.
- **Coral Ridge** `[verified]` — One of the city's oldest neighborhoods (HOA founded 1949): a mix of mid-century single-family homes, waterfront estates east of Bayview Drive with no-fixed-bridge ocean access, and new construction.
- **Victoria Park** `[verified]` — Neighborhood just north of downtown with early-1900s historic homes, mature tree canopy, and proximity to parks and Las Olas.
- **Sailboat Bend** `[verified]` — Historic district along the New River with cottages and older homes; the city's officially designated historic district (550+ buildings, early-1900s–1950s).
- **Tarpon River** `[verified]` — Quiet neighborhood along the Tarpon and New Rivers with a mix of historic Florida cottages, single-family homes, and townhouses.
- **Lauderdale Harbours** `[verified]` — Canal-front neighborhood with Spanish-influenced homes along the Stranahan River.
- **Bay Colony** `[verified]` — Gated waterfront community along the Intracoastal Waterway with deep/wide boating canals, direct (no-fixed-bridge) ocean access, and 24-hour security (stated as a factual physical amenity only).
- **Downtown Fort Lauderdale** `[verified]` — Urban core along the New River anchored by the Riverwalk linear park, high-rise condos, museums, and dining.
