## Page purpose

The Fort Lauderdale page is the operational anchor for Mia's South Florida luxury / waterfront practice. It must:

1. Be the page a serious waterfront buyer or seller would forward to their attorney or financial advisor with the question, "what should I be asking?"
2. Stand on research-backed, hedged-correctly facts — not a rewrite of generic SEO realtor copy.
3. Resolve the city into the four-or-five sub-markets a serious buyer is actually deciding between, without flattening them.
4. Frame the comparison cohort (FtLaud vs. Boca, Palm Beach, Delray, Pompano Beach, Lighthouse Point, Hillsboro Mile) as editorial decision logic, not a list dump.
5. Lead the reader from "I am thinking about Fort Lauderdale" → "I want to send Mia a private brief" without a hard sell, an automated valuation, or an invented inventory promise.

## ICP segments

The Fort Lauderdale page serves four ICP segments, in priority order:

| ICP | Profile | Decision question |
|---|---|---|
| **HNW waterfront primary buyer** | Multi-million-dollar deepwater single-family on a finger isle or canal | "Which canal, which lot, which dock? Is the residence underwritable?" |
| **HNW relocation primary buyer** | Northeast / Midwest / West Coast HNW buyer trading up or seasonally relocating | "How does FtLaud compare to Boca / Palm Beach? What's the right neighborhood given my vessel and lifestyle?" |
| **Waterfront seller (residence-specific)** | Long-time owner positioning a waterfront residence for sale | "How do I price against the right comparable cohort? What documentation (dock, seawall, insurance) earns the right offer?" |
| **In-town / beach-corridor residential** | Walkable Las Olas / Victoria Park / beach-corridor condominium buyer | "What's the lifestyle trade vs. waterfront, vs. Boca beach corridor, vs. Delray Atlantic Avenue?" |

Each segment must find an answer to its dominant decision question on the page.

## ICP questions (mission Phase 3 — eight required answers)

### 1. Why Fort Lauderdale for luxury/waterfront real estate?

Answer must be research-backed: 165 miles of inland waterways within city limits (Source A3 [MED] — hedged), Venice of America + Yachting Capital positioning (Source A1, A7 [HIGH]), deepwater Intracoastal access via Port Everglades inlet (Source A4 [HIGH]), 24 miles of regional Atlantic coastline (Source A1 [HIGH]), $18.5B regional marine industry (Source A7 [HIGH]) — all geographically scoped honestly. The page must not claim "Fort Lauderdale has 300 miles of waterways" — that figure is Broward County, not city.

### 2. How do the city's waterways, canals, Intracoastal access, New River, beach, and marine infrastructure affect property choice?

Answer: 10% of the city's total area (3.8 of 38.6 sq mi) is water (Source A6 [HIGH]); Intracoastal runs N-S providing sheltered passage to the Atlantic via the Port Everglades inlet (Source A4 [HIGH]); the New River runs ~3 miles through downtown — connecting to the Intracoastal — but has multiple drawbridges (do NOT claim "no fixed bridges" for the New River); the LauderGO Water Trolley is a free city-operated public water shuttle (Source A11 [HIGH]); marine industry depth means specialists (surveyors, marine contractors, insurance brokers) are locally abundant.

### 3. How should a buyer compare Fort Lauderdale to its peers?

A 13-market editorial comparison framework, organized by the actual decision logic:

- **Eastern FtLaud finger-isle peers** — Las Olas Isles, Seven Isles, Harbor Beach, Rio Vista, Coral Ridge, Victoria Park, Bay Colony, Bermuda Riviera (the "what eastern FtLaud sub-market" question)
- **Northern Broward waterfront alternatives** — Lighthouse Point, Hillsboro Mile, Pompano Beach (the "should I be looking north of FtLaud" question)
- **Palm Beach County peers** — Boca Raton, Palm Beach, Delray Beach (the "should I be looking north of Broward" question)

Each entry frames one decision-context line ("Comes up when…") rather than a list dump.

### 4. What does a serious waterfront buyer need to know before touring?

The expanded due-diligence framework (V4 expansion of the V3 7-card framework). The serious buyer needs:

- Dock length, beam clearance, fendering, pile condition (from survey + permits, NOT listing photos)
- Seawall age, last inspection, cap condition, tieback integrity, prior remediation
- Bridge clearance + route-to-inlet (vessel-specific)
- Lot orientation: point, finger, canal-wide, lakefront; canal mouth opening
- Canal width / turning basin (NEW V4 — explicit card, was implicit in V3)
- Outdoor living + outdoor kitchen + dock + lift (NEW V4 — explicit card)
- Architectural era + renovation history + permit history
- Flood zone (FEMA), elevation certificate
- Insurance underwriting + 4-point inspection sequence + wind mitigation + prior-claim history (V3 emphasized card, retained as the page's most important question)
- Marine contractor / surveyor / insurance broker introductions before the inspection-window clock starts

### 5. What does a luxury/waterfront seller need to document before valuation or listing?

The expanded seller framework (V4 expansion of V3 5-step playbook):

- Property-specific valuation drawn from the right comparable cohort (NOT a public AVM)
- Comparable-sales cohort selection — same canal, same lot profile, same architectural era
- Dock + seawall documentation (permits, inspections, recent remediation)
- Insurance + flood + elevation documentation (recent 4-point, wind mitigation, elevation certificate, prior-claim history) — NEW V4 explicit step
- Photography + narrative tuned to ONE buyer profile (yachting, walkable, beach)
- Controlled-introduction strategy where the seller wants discretion (with no overclaim about "exclusive private inventory")
- Closing coordination — handoff points between attorney, surveyor, marine contractor, lender

### 6. How do dockage, seawall condition, water depth, bridge clearance, route-to-inlet, canal width, lot orientation, flood/elevation, insurance, and inspection sequence affect decisions?

Answered IN the V4 due-diligence framework above (combined). Each card on the page is one variable, in plain decision-relevant language — what changes the price, what changes the lifestyle, what changes the financability. Marine contractor / surveyor / insurance broker disclaimers explicit at the bottom of the framework (already in V3).

### 7. What should not be trusted from generic automated valuations?

The V3 page already cross-links the existing Insights brief (`/insights/why-automated-valuations-miss-luxury-waterfront/`). V4 keeps the cross-link in the seller playbook anti-pattern aside and adds a buyer-side reference ("If a public estimate is your starting point, treat it as a sanity check on the cohort, not a price").

### 8. What private conversation should the visitor request next?

The V4 page closes with the existing 4-CTA strip (private consultation, confidential valuation, private buyer brief, waterfront review) but also makes the buyer-brief and seller-brief paths discoverable from inside their respective playbooks (the existing aside CTAs). No new CTA primitives — just consistent affordances.
